## 🔴 The headline: the survivor call rests on a false premise

`050` and `059` are **not** two attempts at the same thing. They are **complementary subsystems**
that diverged from a common base and stopped on the same day. Choosing one and discarding the
other does not lose a duplicate — it loses a different half.

| | `050-full-gleam-combined` | `059-full-scope-gleam-glp-implementation` |
|---|---|---|
| commits ahead of `develop` | 48 | 32 |
| commits ahead of their shared base `ed4806ab` (2026-07-20) | 48 | **81** |
| last commit | 2026-07-29 | 2026-07-29 |
| `glp_gleam` files touched | 42 (+8 427 / −16) | 72 (+7 669 / −89) |
| files unique to it in `glp_gleam` | 27 | 57 |
| **what it uniquely contains** | QUIC transport + link lifecycle | compiler / type-checker / bytecode |

Neither is an ancestor of the other. The overlap is **15 files** — the engine core
(`engine.gleam`, `kernels`, `runner`, `scheduler`) and the `link/primitives/*` set.

**`059` uniquely holds the language-implementation stack:** `compiler/codegen.gleam`,
`compiler/loader.gleam`, `compiler/module_hierarchy.gleam`, `analysis/type_checker/type_checker.gleam`,
`analysis/type_checker/program_dfa.gleam`, `bytecode/program.gleam`, `engine/module_runtime.gleam`,
`engine/types.gleam`, `link/reliability/cycle_guard.gleam`, plus a **Lean proof**
(`lean/DistDerefConvergence/.../Basic.lean`, 512 lines) and adversarial convergence tests.

**`050` uniquely holds the distributed transport layer:** `link/transports/quic_ws.gleam`,
`glp_link_quic_ffi.erl`, `link/primitives/link_establish.gleam`, `link_teardown.gleam`,
`link/dist_unify.gleam`, `link/quiescence.gleam`, `link/primitives/capability_gate.gleam`,
`mad/mad_mesh.gleam`, plus their tests.

So the honest form of the question is not *which one survives* but **whether both are integrated
(resolving a 15-file overlap in the engine core) or both are abandoned.** Answering the question
as posed would silently discard a whole subsystem either way.

## Both branches' apparent size is almost entirely noise

The "248 files / 135 K insertions" and "1152 files / 375 K insertions" figures that made these look
enormous do not survive inspection:

- **Roadmap-sync exports dominate both.** Single generated `.specify/roadmap-sync/exports/*.json`
  snapshots of ~41 000 lines each account for the great majority of both insertion counts. They are
  machine-written sync artifacts, not work.
- **`050` has committed build output.** 1 042 of its 1 152 files are under
  `gleam_quic/profile_c/`, and of those **679 are `.obj` files** inside
  `_build/default/lib/...`, alongside vendored `quicer` C sources, CMake files and `.asm`.
  `develop`'s `.gitignore` has no `_build` or `*.obj` rule, which is why they landed.
- **`059` carries 125 COOP mailbox files** (`COOP/olamnit`, `COOP/gavri`) — coordination chatter,
  not code.

Excluding artifacts and chatter, the real deliverable on each side is the `glp_gleam` work in the
table above: roughly 8 K added lines each, `059` touching a broader surface and revising existing
code more (−89 vs −16 deleted lines).

## Recommendation

1. **Drop `058` now** — snapshot commit, archive-tagged, nothing at stake.
2. **Do not answer "050 or 059" as asked.** Put the complementarity above to the engineer: the

   If forced to pick one line to carry forward, `059` is the larger and deeper piece — compiler,
