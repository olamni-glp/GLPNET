Central Package Management is necessary but NOT sufficient, and a .NET-only reading of it would have missed most of the defect. MEASURED in GLPNET 2026-09-01 across six ecosystems in ONE repo: the multi-version drift that motivated ruling Q-shiras-36 was live in FOUR of them simultaneously - .NET (xunit 2.9.2/2.9.3, Test.Sdk 17.11.1/17.14.1, runner.visualstudio 2.8.2/3.1.4), Dart (path ^1.8.0/^1.9.0), Gleam (gleam_stdlib >=0.34/>=0.44) and npm (@electric-sql/pglite 0.2.17 vs 0.4.5 - this repo's CANONICAL DATA LAYER, the single-writer bridge every buildkit tool serialises on). CPM was adopted here and fixed the .NET column only. TWO further gaps measured: (1) CPM pins DIRECT versions and emits no lockfile - packages.lock.json count is ZERO after adoption, so transitive resolution still floats; 14 of 60 manifests across the repo carry no lock at all. (2) A dependency can float WITHOUT a floating version string: rebar.config declares erlzmq at {git, url, {branch, master}} - unpinned, outside any registry, and invisible to both a version-string scan and a PURL-registry-only coordinate model. The ban must be restated over REFERENCES (exact version OR commit SHA), not version strings. Also settled two rows the fleet had UNCOVERED: GLP has modules not packages (1184 .glp, 77 -module decls, 218 imported-procedure decls, ZERO version directives, resolution by self.glp directory scope chain) and cannot be given a manifest without a 1.14 language-authority decision from Udi; AtomVM is a runtime/build target, not a registry.
